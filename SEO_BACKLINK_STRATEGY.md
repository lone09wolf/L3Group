# L3 Group: practical backlink plan

No backlinks have been created or claimed as part of this work.

| Opportunity | Recommended action | Best destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing clients and project partners | Ask permission to publish a short, factual project story. Offer the partner a useful link to the relevant photo collection if they feature the completed work. | The relevant `/projects/...` collection |
| Architects, engineers and suppliers already involved in L3 work | Propose a joint case study with credited photographs, actual responsibilities and an original explanation of a construction challenge. Let each partner decide whether to reference it. | A project collection or future approved case study |
| Trade bodies and local business associations | Verify actual membership and eligibility first, then request an accurate website link from the organisation's official member directory. Displaying a logo alone is not evidence of membership. | `/about` or `/` |
| Local publications and community organisations | Pitch a genuinely newsworthy completed project, apprenticeship or community contribution, only where facts and permissions can be documented. | The corresponding factual project story |
| Unlinked mentions | Review existing mentions of the company and politely request a link where it would help readers identify L3 Group. | `/` |
| Google Business Profile | Create or verify an eligible service-area business profile; keep the business name, website, phone and service area accurate. Do not invent an office address. This supports discovery, not a guaranteed ranking backlink. | `/` |

## First month

1. Confirm project ownership, photography permissions, partner names and any directory memberships.
2. Write two useful case studies from actual work: scope, constraints, L3's role, solution and photographs. Do not invent project dates, outcomes, credentials or client quotes.
3. Contact a small set of existing business relationships individually with a relevant page, not a mass-mail template.
4. Track the referring page, linked destination, permission, referral enquiries and Search Console Links report. Measure relevance and real visitors, not raw link counts.

Avoid purchased ranking links, link exchanges at scale, automated directory submissions and keyword-stuffed anchors. Paid placements must be clearly disclosed and appropriately qualified by the publisher. See [Google's link-spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#link-spam) and [Google Business Profile guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091).
